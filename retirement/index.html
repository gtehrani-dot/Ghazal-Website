Virginia's rule on growth

Under Va. Code § 20-107.3, Virginia applies the source-of-funds rule — retirement value earned before marriage is separate property, and passive (market-driven) appreciation on that separate share stays separate even when it occurs during the marriage. BAH sat untouched with no active management by either spouse, so its full documented growth — including the 2011–2014 stretch, before it merged with DL — stays premarital. Separately, Va. Code § 20-107.3(G) caps what a non-owning spouse may be awarded from the marital share of a retirement account at 50% of that share.

Because the BAH, DL, and FranConnect rollovers were pooled into the same investment lineup once inside the IRA, every dollar in the account earned the identical percentage gain or loss during any period they were together. That's what makes a pro-rata (“coverture fraction”) tracing approach both mathematically sound and easy to verify against the statements: once a premarital/marital ownership percentage is established at the moment funds combine, that same percentage carries forward automatically as the whole account rises or falls.

See Section 5 (Summary) for the bottom-line split, and Section 3 for open items to confirm with counsel.

Assumptions to Confirm with Counsel / Mediator Before Finalizing

  • Valuation date: this can be updated at the time of divorce. This proposal uses the 6/30/2026 Fidelity statement as a placeholder — the model updates cleanly to whichever date ends up controlling (date of separation, date of filing, or another negotiated date).
  • DL 401(k) growth, 2011–2014: this pro-rata growth-splitting step only had to be applied to DL, since BAH is 100% premarital and FranConnect is 100% marital — neither needed its own internal growth split. For DL, rather than treating its entire increase in value as marital, this proposal traces DL's own real growth ($10,787.51, net of fees) proportionally between its premarital principal and its marital contributions, based on their actual share of DL's basis (37.14% / 62.86%). A more granular alternative would match each individual paycheck contribution to the fund performance from its specific contribution date forward, rather than using one blended proportion for the whole 2011–2014 period; given all contributions sat in the same pooled holdings throughout, the practical difference is likely small, but your attorney may want that finer cut if precision here is contested.
  • Treatment of passive appreciation on separate property: Virginia follows the source-of-funds rule (Va. Code § 20-107.3), under which passive, market-driven growth on separate property stays separate even when it occurs during the marriage. That's the rule this proposal applies to BAH's growth. It's worth confirming with your attorney that a court would characterize BAH's growth as passive (no active management by either spouse) rather than active — that's the one fact question underlying this legal conclusion. Separately, Va. Code § 20-107.3(G) caps what a non-owning spouse may be awarded from the marital share of a retirement account at 50% of that share.
  • Ben's SEP-IRA: this is a separate account from the IRA divided in this proposal (different account number, different IRA type, never merged in) — see the Three Funding Sources section. Ben's own emails show the SEP-IRA was already active by January 2013 and had been for “the two years” prior, meaning it was opened in 2011 — the year of the marriage.
